Part II · What light is · Chapter 6

Colour

Chapter 5 measured how bright light is. A light also has a colour, and a renderer has to carry it on every ray. ShadyTracer carries three numbers a ray, red, green and blue. This chapter is why three are enough for the eye, what they mean exactly, why one of them can be negative, and how a light given the way a lamp's datasheet gives it (a colour temperature, or a point on a chart) becomes those three.

1Three numbers for a colour

Light comes in wavelengths, from violet at about 380 nanometres to deep red at about 780, and a light's spectrum says how much of it there is at each. The eye does not see the spectrum. It has three kinds of cone cell, each sensitive to a broad range of wavelengths, and all it keeps of a light is how strongly each kind responds: three numbers. Two lights with different spectra that give the same three look exactly the same.

In 1931 the CIE, the body that sets these standards, fixed the three, from experiments in which people matched colours, as colour-matching functions: x̄, ȳ and z̄, the three curves in the figure. Weigh a spectrum by each curve and add up over the wavelengths, and you have its X, Y and Z, the numbers every colour standard since is built on. ȳ is the eye's sensitivity to brightness, so Y is chapter 5's luminance. Here the light is a narrow band of green at 530 nm (the dashed curve, its shape only): mostly ȳ, a little x̄. Slide its wavelength along by its handle.

Now a hot body, the glow of something heated (a lamp's filament, the sun), at 6500 K: a broad spectrum over every wavelength, and three numbers close to equal. That is about what daylight's white is. Its exact red, green and blue (the next section says what those are) come out 1.042, 0.984 and 1.034.

At 2700 K, a warm lamp, the spectrum leans to the red, and so does its colour: X is 1.11 of Y, Z only 0.32.

The curves here are not the CIE's table but a fit to it, a few bell curves added together, which ShadyTracer uses for its coloured coatings and prisms (chapters 10 and 11); its test holds it against published values of the table. A fit has errors: a hot body at 3000 K through these curves reads 3024 K, Duv +0.0004, on the standard's line of hot bodies that the next sections use.

2A map of colours

X, Y and Z mix colour and brightness. Divide each by their sum, and the brightness drops out: x = X / (X + Y + Z) and y = Y / (X + Y + Z) are a light's chromaticity, and every colour is one point on this map. Round its curved edge are the single wavelengths of the first figure, 460 nm to 620 marked; every real light lies inside, since it is a sum of them. Two lights mixed lie on the straight line between their points.

A screen makes colours from three lights of its own, its primaries: the dashed triangle is those of Rec.709, the standard ShadyTracer's red, green and blue are. The screen can show only what lies inside its triangle, its gamut. The orange line is where the hot bodies lie, 1000 K at the red end to 15,000 K at the blue, and the ringed dot is D65, the standard daylight white a Rec.709 screen shows for equal red, green and blue. Drag the point. On the right, its red, green and blue at luminance 1, and below them what the screen shows.

The point is now on the hot bodies' line at 3000 K. A light near the line has a colour temperature: the temperature of the nearest hot body. How far it is from the line is its Duv, measured square to the line on an older map, CIE 1960's, where equal distances look more nearly equally different. This is how lamps are specified: "3000 K, Duv 0".

Here, 4000 K at a Duv of +0.02: off the line towards green, a colour a lighting designer would call greenish and a standard would reject (they allow a few thousandths).

And here a royal-blue LED, a colour lamps really use, far outside the triangle. Its red, green and blue at luminance 1 are 1.45, −2.57 and 35.0. A negative green: no amount of the screen's three lights mixes to it, and the sum needs green taken away. ShadyTracer keeps that negative number on the ray. Every material multiplies each channel by a number of 0 or more, which keeps sums of colours exact, so a detector turns the three back into exactly the X, Y and Z the LED was given. Only the screen cannot show it, and clips it at the very end, keeping its luminance: 1.13, 0 and 10.5.

The mathsFrom a spectrum to the screen's red, green and blue

With S(λ) the spectrum, X, Y and Z are sums over the wavelengths, every nanometre here:

X = Σ S(λ) x̄(λ),   Y = Σ S(λ) ȳ(λ),   Z = Σ S(λ) z̄(λ)

Rec.709's red, green and blue are three particular X, Y, Z triples, the columns of a 3 × 3 matrix M: a colour R, G, B is M times (R, G, B) in XYZ. Going back, (R, G, B) = M⁻¹ (X, Y, Z), and nothing in that stops a channel being negative. The middle row of M is Y: 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B, chapter 5's luminance. From a chromaticity and a luminance Y back to XYZ: X = x Y / y, Z = (1 − x − y) Y / y.

Because all of it is a matrix times a vector, it is linear: the XYZ of two lights added is their XYZs added, and so are their R, G, B. That is what makes a negative channel safe to carry, and mixing exact (section 3).

3Mixing lights

A white LED lamp is often three or more coloured LEDs mixed, and the question is how much of each. Because colours add as XYZ, it is one sum: the three LEDs' XYZ per lumen, times the lumens of each, must be the target's XYZ. Three unknowns, three equations, solved at once. These are the LEDs of the user's own luminaire, a red, a green and a royal blue (the handles), mixed to 4000 K at 1000 lumens: 310.5 lumens of red, 673.0 of green and 16.5 of blue, the split the user's own colour tool gave.

Cooler, 6500 K: more blue (30.8 lm) and green, less red. Drag an LED and the triangle and the split move with it.

Warmer, 1500 K: the target has moved outside the triangle, below the line from red to green. The solve still answers, with −0.6 lumens of blue. No LED gives negative light: three lights can mix only to colours inside their triangle.

5Tricks and trade-offs

    
Each is stamped with how it is known; point at a stamp to see what it means.

    

      
Three channels, not a wavelength per ray. A renderer could trace one wavelength at a time and
        turn it into a colour at the end. ShadyTracer chose not to: "a white source traced one wavelength at a time is
        colour noise everywhere" (roadmap/PLAN.md, Colour, the hybrid, agreed 2026-09-27). Three
        channels are exact for a light given by its chromaticity, through materials that treat the three alike. Only
        where a material sorts wavelengths, a prism or a coloured coating, does a ray take a wavelength (chapter
        11).

    

    

      
Exact, measured. A detector in the optics trace read back the colour each light was given
        (the optics-colour test, the user's Pascal card, 2026-09-27, docs/TESTING.md): a 3000 K
        light to 9 × 10⁻¹¹ in u′v′, a 6500 K one through glass 5 × 10⁻⁹, the royal blue 8 × 10⁻⁹. The LED mixes read
        0.0012 and 0.0005, the counting noise of about 1650 blue rays: each ray comes from one LED, picked at random,
        and the blue one gives few.

    

    

      
A colour converges after its brightness. The same three LEDs mixed to 4000 K, lighting a
        floor in the picture (the optics-light test, Pascal): the first press read the right lux but 4247
        K, from a sensor 3 × 3 pixels wide that had caught about 17 blue photons, where one standard deviation is 250 K.
        Read over the whole frame, about a million hits, it gave 3999.9 K.

    

  






  

    
6What went wrong before

    
    
      
  • Every photon clipped. Light carried by photons was clipped into the screen's gamut as each one
        was added to a pixel. A mix of saturated LEDs is white only if their exact colours add up; clipped one by one,
        each lost its negative channel, and the mix shifted. Now only the screen clips, once
        (roadmap/PLAN.md, Colour, the hybrid).
    • 
      
  • A coloured light that lit its scene white. The table of light sources the optics trace reads
        left the colour out (the comment in builder/sources.js).
    • 
      
  • A Duv that was not one. The colour tool ShadyTracer's formulas came from measured Duv in u′v′,
        the later map where v′ is 1.5 times the 1960 map's v, so its "0.02" was not a Duv of 0.02; ShadyTracer measures
        it in 1960 uv, as the standard does (colorimetry.js's header).
    • 
      
  • A colour that could not be specified. A light used to be a red, green and blue and a
        luminance: "fine for a picture and wrong for LED work", where a light is a chromaticity and a flux.
