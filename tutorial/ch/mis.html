Part IV · Path tracing · Chapter 15

Two ways to the light

Chapter 14 found light by aiming at it: pick a point on a lamp and send a shadow ray. Chapter 13 found it by chance: bounce the way the surface scatters, and see whether the bounce meets a lamp. Each is good where the other is bad. Multiple importance sampling uses both at once and weighs every answer by how good its way was at finding it, so the picture gets the best of each.

1Each way fails somewhere

Four plates, from nearly a mirror (top row, σ 0.02) to quite rough (bottom row, σ 0.6), and four lamps over them, from tiny to long, each giving the same light in all. A glossy plate reflects light mostly near its mirror direction; σ is how far, in radians, its reflection spreads around it. The camera sees each lamp reflected somewhere along each plate: the tiny lamp near the left end, the long one near the right. Every pixel's light is estimated three ways, from the same random numbers: by picking a point on a lamp (left column), by picking a direction the way the plate reflects (middle), and by both, weighed (right). After 8 samples the three differ a lot.

After 256 samples, look at where each is still speckled. Picking a point on a lamp is noisy on the sharp plates, worst under the long lamp: a point picked anywhere on it is almost never in the narrow range of directions the plate reflects. Picking a direction is noisy on the rough plates, worst under the tiny lamp: a direction drawn from a wide spread almost never meets it. Each is smooth where the other is speckled. Both, weighed is smooth everywhere: the readout's error bars are 13% and 12% of the light for the two alone, 6% with MIS.

The mathsTwo estimates of one integral

The light a plate point sends to the camera is an integral over the directions light arrives from. Either way of picking a direction ω gives an estimate of it:

f(ω) cos θ Le(ω)plamp(ω)   or   f(ω) cos θ Le(ω)pplate(ω)

plamp
how likely picking a point on a lamp is to give direction ω, per radian
pplate
how likely the plate's own reflection is to give it

An estimate is noisy where its p is small but the light is not: a rare direction carrying a lot, divided by a tiny p. MIS adds the two estimates, each multiplied by a weight, and the weights for any one direction add up to 1, so nothing is counted twice or lost. The power heuristic weighs a strategy by the square of its density against the other's:

wlamp = plamp²plamp² + pplate²

So a direction only one strategy was likely to find counts almost entirely through that strategy.

2Who is good at what

At one point of one plate, the top plot shows how likely each strategy is to pick each direction: the lamps' density is flat-topped humps, one for each lamp seen from here, taller where a lamp looks smaller from here (a short lamp, or a far one seen edge-on); the plate's is a bell around its mirror direction. The bottom plot shows the weights MIS gives each. On the rough plate under the tiny lamp, the plate's bell is low and wide and the lamp's hump is tall: the tiny lamp's light counts almost entirely through the lamp strategy (weight 0.99).

On the sharp plate under the long lamp, the plate's bell is a narrow spike far above the long lamp's low hump, and the weights turn round: in the mirror direction, the plate's way counts entirely. Move the point along the plate and change the plate, and watch the weights follow whichever density is higher.

5What went wrong before

    
    
      
  • A glass ball with a plain shadow. A ray refracted out of glass towards the sun was weighed
        against the lamp strategy, whose density the sun's cone gave as not zero there, although the lamp strategy only
        ever aims at directions above the surface. Its weight came out 0; the lamp strategy never produced it either; so
        the sun through glass was counted by neither, and a glass ball cast a plain dark shadow (the comment above
        prevPdf).
    • 
      
  • Glossy plastic lit by chance. A perfectly smooth coat used to be given a density of 10⁹, a
        stand-in for "a single direction". That number also switched off aiming for the matte base under the coat, so a
        glossy plastic in sunlight was lit only by bounces that happened to find the sun (the comment on delta
        events).
