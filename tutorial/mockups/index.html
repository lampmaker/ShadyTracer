ShadyTracer, inside out · style mockups

Four ways it could look

The same pages (the contents, the glossary and three chapters) in four styles, each with a light and a dark version. Every card below is drawn in its own style, figure included. Open one, read a little, drag something, press Dark at the top right, and switch between A, B, C and D there: the page stays where it is and only the style changes.

A · Lab notebook

An engineer's pad

Squared green paper · fountain pen · red pencil

Headings and figure labels written by hand, the text in a book face so it stays easy to read; lines wobble a little and are drawn twice, like ink. Dark: a slate page and white gel pen.

  • Closest to "hand-drawn, technical, on paper"
  • Warm and approachable for a first-time reader
  • Figures look sketched, so a rough one does not look broken
  • Handwritten headings can tire over 34 chapters
  • Wobbly lines are a little less exact to read off

B · Blueprint

Drawing office

Cyanotype · white lines · stencil lettering

A fine grid, a ruled border, a title block, stencil headings and the mono lettering of a drawing office. In dark it is a real blueprint (white on Prussian blue); in light, a whiteprint (blue-violet on pale paper).

  • Unmistakably technical; the dark version is striking
  • Grid and mono labels suit measured diagrams
  • The light version is the quieter of the two
  • Long reading on blue is harder than on paper

C · Drafting sheet

A patent drawing

Vellum · black ink · one vermilion

Crisp lines, hatching, FIG. numbers, a ruled border with zone marks, small-capital headings and typewritten labels. One spot colour, for light. Dark: white ink on charcoal.

  • The most exact: nothing wobbles, everything reads off cleanly
  • Calm over many chapters; scales to dense pages
  • Less playful; closest to a classic textbook
  • Rays mostly black: told apart by dashes, not colour

D · Risograph (wildcard)

A printed zine

Grainy stock · fluorescent pink and blue

Two inks printed one over the other, a little out of register; bold grotesque headings and a very legible body face; solids printed as dots. Dark: the same inks on black stock.

  • The most fun; strong for a school audience
  • Colours carry meaning: pink is light, blue the camera
  • Least "technical drawing"
  • Fluorescent colours can tire the eye in long reads

A recommendation, for you to overrule

A, the lab notebook, because it answers all three words of the brief at once: hand-drawn, technical, paper. Two things from C are worth taking into it whichever you choose: numbered FIG. captions and the title block, which make a chapter feel like a drawing set rather than a web page. B is the one to pick if you want the tutorial to feel like engineering first; D if the readers are mostly students and you want them to enjoy it. A mix is possible: the styles share every component and differ only in their tokens.

The same in all four

The layout: a sheet with a title block, text beside a figure that stays in place while its paragraphs scroll by, fold-outs for the maths, the three stamps for how sure a claim is, excerpts read live from the code, glossary cards, prev and next.

The figures: one drawing kit (tutorial/kit/pen.js) draws every line, ray, shape and label, taking its colours, faces and line character from the style. A style is one CSS file of tokens; the choice deletes the other three.

Fonts come from Google Fonts for this round only. The chosen style's fonts will be kept in the repository, so the finished tutorial loads nothing from elsewhere.

This page and the style strip go once a style is chosen. Nothing in the tutorial is part of the application: it lives in tutorial/ and is served beside it.
