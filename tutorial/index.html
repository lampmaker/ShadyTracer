A tutorial beside the program

ShadyTracer, inside out

How a path tracer that runs in your browser turns shapes and lamps into a picture: rays, distances, light, glass, noise, photons, haze, the graphics card and every trick that makes it fast. For anyone who wants to understand ShadyTracer's code, build their own renderer, or improve this one. No ray tracing, optics or GPU programming needed to start.

◇The map

Thirty-four chapters in ten parts, in reading order. The filled stations are written (this is the first round, for judging the direction); the rest are planned, each with the one line it will cover. Every chapter has the same shape: the question, the idea with a live figure, the maths in a fold-out, the real code, the tricks and what they cost, what went wrong before, and things to try.

◇Reading the figures

Every figure is drawn in a flat world: a slice of a scene, so it can be drawn and dragged. The same colour means the same thing in every one. Circled crosses are handles: drag them. Paragraphs with a dot beside them move the figure next to them when you scroll to them (or click them).

Words with a dotted underline are in the glossary; hover or tap one for a short explanation. Code shown on a page is read from ShadyTracer's own files when you open the page, with a link to the exact lines.
